## General Recommendations (Regardless of Specific Discussion)

Based on current AI trends and the fact that a Grok discussion is being surfaced as relevant:

### 1. Strengthen Multi-Platform Competency

**Current State:**
- Curriculum may implicitly favor certain AI platforms (e.g., OpenAI due to market dominance)
- Students may become overly dependent on single platform

**Recommended Actions:**

**Immediate (This Week):**
- [ ] Audit current curriculum for platform assumptions
- [ ] Identify where we're inadvertently teaching platform-specific vs. portable skills
- [ ] Document which platforms are used in which courses

**Short-term (This Month):**
- [ ] Add "multi-platform strategy" section to A²I 601 syllabus
- [ ] Create comparison matrix of major AI platforms (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, etc.)
- [ ] Develop vendor evaluation rubric that students can apply to any platform

**Medium-term (This Semester):**
- [ ] Update project guidelines to encourage platform diversity
- [ ] Create labs that explicitly compare different AI platforms
- [ ] Faculty workshop on tool-agnostic teaching approaches

**Long-term (Next Cycle):**
- [ ] Consider "AI Platform Evaluation" as formal learning outcome
- [ ] Develop partnerships with multiple AI providers (not just one)
- [ ] Research program's approach to platform diversity

**Alignment Check:**
✅ Strongly aligns with "coherence is scarce" - orchestrating multiple platforms is harder than using one
✅ Prepares students for real-world multi-vendor environments
✅ Reduces program's dependency on any single AI company
✅ Future-proofs learning (platforms will continue to evolve)

### 2. Emphasize Critical AI Evaluation

**Current State:**
- Students may treat all AI outputs as equally reliable
- Limited focus on comparing and validating AI responses

**Recommended Actions:**

**Add to Multiple Courses:**
- **A²I 601**: Vendor evaluation and due diligence
- **A²I 603**: Evidence quality and source validation
- **A²I 606**: Governance across diverse AI platforms
- **A²I 623**: Systematic evaluation methodologies

**Key Skills to Strengthen:**
1. **Source Validation**: Understanding where AI information comes from
2. **Cross-Platform Verification**: Using multiple AIs to validate critical decisions
3. **Capability Boundaries**: Knowing what each platform does well/poorly
4. **Bias Awareness**: Recognizing platform-specific biases and limitations
5. **Cost-Benefit Analysis**: When to use which tool for which purpose

**Integration Approach:**
- Create "AI Response Analysis" exercises across multiple courses
- Assign projects requiring students to justify their platform choice
- Develop rubrics that reward thoughtful tool selection
- Include "why I used this AI" in assignment requirements

### 3. Update Industry Scan and Monitoring

**Action Items:**

### 4. Enhance Real-World Workplace Preparation

**Observation:** Professional environments increasingly involve:
- Multiple AI platforms available
- Different tools for different purposes
- Evolving internal AI policies
- Need for consistent judgment across tools

**Curriculum Response:**

**A²I 608 (Leading AI Transformation):**
- Add module: "Building AI-Mature Organizations"
- Topics: Tool selection, policy development, change management
- Assignment: Create AI adoption strategy for fictional organization

**Across All Studios:**
- Require students to document their AI tool choices
- Evaluate based on appropriateness of tool selection
- Simulate enterprise constraints (budget, compliance, vendor relationships)

**Capstone Enhancement:**
- Require multi-platform analysis in capstone proposals
- Include vendor selection justification
- Address governance across chosen platforms

### 5. Clarify Program AI Use Policy

**Issue:** With proliferating AI platforms, need clear guidance on:
- Which AI tools students may/should use
- How to cite and attribute AI contributions
- What level of AI assistance is appropriate
- How AI-augmented work will be assessed

**Recommended Policy Components:**

**1. Permitted Tools**
- Students may use any commercially available AI platform
- Must document which tools were used and how
- Must be able to explain and defend AI-generated content
- Final submission must reflect student's own judgment

**2. Required Disclosure**
- Statement of AI use with each major assignment
- Description of what AI did vs. what student did
- Reflection on AI's contribution to learning

**3. Assessment Approach**
- Evaluate student's judgment in using AI, not just outputs
- Credit for effective AI orchestration
- Assess understanding demonstrated, not just correctness
- Oral exams/discussions to validate understanding

**4. Professional Standards**
- Treat as professional skill development
- Teach industry best practices for AI use
- Emphasize transparency and accountability
- Prepare for workplace AI expectations

**Implementation:**
- Draft policy document for faculty review
- Student handbook section on AI use
- Include in course syllabi
- Revisit and update annually

## Appendix: Current AI Platform Landscape (November 2025)

### Major Conversational AI Platforms

**For student reference and curriculum development:**

| Platform | Company | Key Strengths | Primary Use Cases | Business Model |
|----------|---------|---------------|-------------------|----------------|
| **ChatGPT** | OpenAI | Most popular, broad capabilities, GPT-4 | General purpose, coding, writing | Freemium + Enterprise |
| **Claude** | Anthropic | Longer context, safety focus, analysis | Document analysis, research, coding | Freemium + Enterprise |
| **Gemini** | Google | Google integration, multimodal | Search, productivity, creative | Freemium + Workspace integration |
| **Grok** | xAI | Real-time data, X integration, casual tone | Current events, social context | X Premium subscription |
| **Copilot** | Microsoft | Microsoft 365 integration, enterprise | Productivity, collaboration | Microsoft 365 subscription |
| **Llama** | Meta | Open source, customizable | Custom deployments, research | Open source (hosted or self-deploy) |

**Industry-Specific:**
- **Healthcare**: Specialized medical AI assistants
- **Legal**: Contract analysis and legal research tools
- **Finance**: Financial analysis and compliance tools
- **Code**: GitHub Copilot, Replit Ghostwriter, etc.

### Platform Selection Considerations

**For teaching students to evaluate:**

1. **Capability Match**
   - What task are you trying to accomplish?
   - Which platform's strengths align with your needs?

2. **Data Considerations**
   - What data will you share with the AI?
   - What are the privacy/security requirements?
   - Is real-time information important?

3. **Integration Needs**
   - What tools/platforms does it need to work with?
   - API availability and quality?
   - Workflow integration?

4. **Cost Structure**
   - Free tier limitations?
   - Pricing model (per query, subscription, enterprise)?
   - Total cost of ownership?

5. **Governance & Compliance**
   - Terms of service and usage rights?
   - Data retention and training policies?
   - Regulatory compliance (GDPR, HIPAA, etc.)?

6. **Reliability & Support**
   - Uptime and performance?
   - Version stability?
   - Support options?

**Pedagogical Approach:**
Rather than teaching students to use specific platforms, teach them this evaluation framework so they can assess any current or future AI tool.
